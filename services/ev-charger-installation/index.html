EV charger installation in Pickering.

Installing an EV charger in Pickering when one party rents and another owns raises questions about permission, ownership and payment. A vehicle delivery date makes those questions urgent, so settle them in writing first.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Pickering property

Write down who owns the home, who occupies it and where the vehicle will park. Gather the charger model if chosen and any written permission already given by the landlord or condominium corporation.

Ask who will own the charger if the tenant leaves or the home is sold, and which party the contractor needs written approval from. Ask how charging use will be tracked between the parties.

What should the assessment establish?

With the assessment done, the party who authorizes the work decides whether a charger for a Pickering tenant and landlord goes ahead, and when. That decision rests on a scope naming the supply the charger would use, whether that supply can carry it and whether the circuit is dedicated, and how the equipment suits an outdoor or garage spot. That party appears there too, with whoever answers for the equipment afterward and what becomes of the installation when the tenancy ends. If a vehicle delivery date is fixed, steps that depend on the notification or a utility review are marked.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a renter need the owner's consent before a charger goes in?

Permission should be settled in writing before any work starts. The landlord, or a condominium corporation where one applies, may need to approve the location and the electrical work, and the contractor may ask to see that approval.

Does a shared parking spot change how a charger is installed?

It can. The distance from the supply, the route for the cable and who has access to the spot all affect the design. Describe the spot and the route in words, and let the contractor assess what is suitable.
