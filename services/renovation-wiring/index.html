Renovation wiring in Pickering.

Relocating a laundry or mechanical area shifts electrical needs from one spot to another, and a Pickering renovation with a deadline leaves little room for a missed detail. Plan the wiring before demolition starts.

Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your Pickering property

Sketch the current and proposed positions of the washer, dryer, furnace or other equipment, with approximate distances in words. Gather the appliance specifications, the drawings and the schedule of other trades who need the area.

Ask the contractor which existing circuits, boxes and cables in the old location must be removed, capped or reused, and who confirms that before walls are closed. Ask when the ESA notification should be filed.

What should the assessment establish?

What does the laundry or mechanical area supply now, and what will the new location need? The report answers with the circuits for each appliance and the route each one takes. What happens to wiring that is abandoned? Each piece is listed with how it is dealt with and recorded. Where does the electrical stage fall among the other trades? The scope sets out the order of work and when that stage must be finished before surfaces are closed. When a Pickering home is being prepared for sale, a written record shows where each relocated circuit now runs.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does moving a dryer need a new circuit or can it reuse the old one?

That depends on the appliance and the existing circuit, so it should be assessed rather than assumed. Provide the appliance information and let the contractor say whether the old circuit can be reused or must change.

What electrical steps come before walls and floors are closed up?

The electrical stage and any required review should be complete before surfaces cover the work. Ask the contractor to state in writing which electrical steps must come before other trades and which can follow.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Drawings and intended use of each space
  • Appliance and equipment model information
  • Construction schedule and other trades
  • Existing installation records if available
  • Person authorized to approve changes

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
