Before work begins

Work backwards from the date the project must meet.

Unlike a home its owners will keep, one being prepared for sale may have a listing date that can limit how much work fits. A buyer or their inspector may also want records of the changes and who made them.

Start from the fixed date and count backwards: gather product sheets and earlier paperwork first, then confirm who can authorize work and open the door. An enquiry does not hold a date, so allow room for assessment and ESA notification.

What records might a buyer ask to see after electrical work?

A buyer or inspector may ask for the ESA notification, the contractor's written scope, the invoice and any inspection paperwork. Keep these together in one folder so they can be handed over without a search.

How do I compare quotes when the work has a hard deadline?

Compare the written scope line by line, not only the total. Check what each quote includes, what it assumes about access and who handles the ESA notification, then ask how the stated timing could change.

Should the circuit be ready before the appliance or vehicle arrives?

Where the supply and equipment allow it, preparing the circuit ahead can avoid a gap, but choose the equipment model first. Ask the contractor to confirm what the installation needs before any delivery is accepted.

Can electrical work start before closing on a home I am buying?

Only someone with authority over the property can approve work on it. Depending on the situation that may involve the seller, a condominium corporation or the lender, so ask the contractor what written authorization is needed first.

Does sending the form arrange anyone to come to my home?

The provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor you deal with confirms directly who it is and its licence, then agrees the job and its price with you, and when any work happens. Sending the form dispatches no one and holds no date: the site publishes guides, takes in enquiries and reviews them, and neither prices nor does the work.
